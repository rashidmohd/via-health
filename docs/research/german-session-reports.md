# Draft the facts, leave judgment to therapists

An AI session report is legally defensible in Germany only when it does two things. It must be a **draft of what was said and done**, which the therapist reviews, edits and signs as their own **Verlaufsdokumentation**. And it must stay out of every clinical judgment: diagnosis, mental-status assessment, risk, formulation and treatment progress. German law (§630f BGB, §9 of the Musterberufsordnung) requires a dated entry for every session, written close to the session. The entry must record the essential interventions and their results, show a hypothesis-driven treatment concept, be kept for 10 years, and remain correctable only with the original still visible. The Bundespsychotherapeutenkammer's (BPtK) own template gives each entry a fixed header and five content blocks. Those blocks split cleanly into descriptive parts an LLM can draft and interpretive parts only the therapist can write. The evidence on ambient scribes points the same way. Errors are rare, about **1.5% hallucinated sentences**, but almost half of them are clinically major, and they cluster in the assessment and plan sections. The BPtK classes transcription and documentation as "administrative KI" that is permitted under conditions. MDR and the EU AI Act both turn on whether the software suggests diagnoses or treatment, or infers emotion. For this app the conclusion is a split template: the AI drafts the descriptive fields with links to their source, and the clinical fields are therapist-only, including suicidality. Sign-off creates a versioned record the client can inspect. The audio and, by default, the transcript are deleted.

## German law makes every session a dated, correctable and inspectable entry

The duty to document comes from civil law and professional law, and the two texts are almost identical. §630f BGB requires a paper or electronic Patientenakte kept "in unmittelbarem zeitlichen Zusammenhang mit der Behandlung". It must record "sämtliche aus fachlicher Sicht … wesentlichen Maßnahmen und deren Ergebnisse", and a correction is allowed only if **the original content and the date of the change stay recognizable, explicitly also in electronic records** ([§630f BGB](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/__630f.html)). §9 MBO repeats this for psychotherapists and extends it to counselling. §9 Abs. 3 MBO and §630f Abs. 3 set **10 years of retention after treatment ends** ([BPtK Muster-Berufsordnung, 15.11.2025](https://api.bptk.de/uploads/Muster_Berufsordnung_der_B_Pt_K_412a6bcb36.pdf)). The PTK Berlin reads "zeitnah" as the same or the following day. It also states that keyword-style notes with abbreviations are enough, as long as a professional can follow them ([PTK Berlin, Dittberner](https://psychotherapeutenkammer-berlin.de/media/727)). No court has defined the time window for psychotherapy. The "same or next day" reading comes from a Kammer lawyer's slides.

The stakes are evidentiary. Under §630h Abs. 3 BGB, an essential measure that was not documented is **presumed not to have been taken** ([§630h BGB](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/__630h.html)). The commentary quoted by PTK NRW puts it as "Was nicht dokumentiert ist, ist nicht gemacht" ([PTK NRW Empfehlungen 2020](https://www.ptk-nrw.de/fileadmin/user_upload/meldungen/2020/Empfehlungen_Dok_Internet.pdf)). In psychotherapy the measures that matter most for liability are suicide-risk assessment, crisis interventions, and Aufklärung and Einwilligung. An AI draft that silently leaves them out transfers that risk to the therapist. The PTK NRW adds a qualitative bar: recording only what the client said is not enough, and "ein hypothesengeleitetes Behandlungskonzept [muss] erkennbar sein". That concept needs to show across the entries taken together, not in every single one ([PTK NRW](https://www.ptk-nrw.de/fileadmin/user_upload/meldungen/2020/Empfehlungen_Dok_Internet.pdf)). A summary of the transcript alone therefore fails the professional standard. The therapist's reasoning has to enter the record somewhere.

The client will be able to read everything. Since 6 February 2026, §630g BGB grants prompt inspection of the complete record, and **the first copy is free**, which implements EuGH C-307/22. Inspection can be refused only for "erhebliche therapeutische Gründe" or significant rights of third parties, and the refusal must be justified ([§630g BGB](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/__630g.html); [LTO on C-307/22](https://www.lto.de/recht/nachrichten/n/EuGH-C30722-Vorlage-Patientenakte-Kopie-Datenschutzgrundverordnung-DSGVO-Vorabentscheidungsverfahren-BGH)). §11 Abs. 1 MBO states that the therapist's documented **subjective impressions are in principle open to inspection** ([BPtK MBO](https://api.bptk.de/uploads/Muster_Berufsordnung_der_B_Pt_K_412a6bcb36.pdf)). The Ärztekammer Bremen notes that the old "doppelte Buchführung", where personal notes could be blacked out, no longer exists since the Patientenrechtegesetz ([ÄK Bremen](https://extras.aekhb.de/data/mediapool/pa_re_einsichtnahme.pdf)). Unlike US HIPAA "psychotherapy notes", there is no protected side channel. Anything the app stores about a client is presumptively accessible under §630g or Art. 15 GDPR, including drafts and transcripts while they exist.

The law does not require the therapist to type the note personally, and it does not require a signature on each entry. The duty rests on "der Behandelnde", however. The Niedersachsen Berufsordnung of April 2026 adds that therapists "behandeln persönlich und eigenverantwortlich" ([PKN BO-Änderung 2026](https://www.pknds.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/20260427_Bekanntmachung_Berufsordnung.pdf)). A machine draft therefore becomes documentation only once the therapist reviews it and adopts it as their own.

## The BPtK template has a fixed header and five content blocks, and it is not SOAP

The closest thing to an official German session template is the "Verlaufsdokumentation" table in the BPtK/PTK NRW recommendations, which the 37th Deutscher Psychotherapeutentag adopted in 2020. Its formal header marks **client name, date, time and session number as "obligatorisch jede Stunde"**. Participants, place and session type are added where relevant. The content blocks are "Thema auf der Erzählebene", "Psychotherapeutisches Verständnis des Themas auf Theorieebene (verfahrensspezifisch)", "Interventionen und Reaktionen", "Veränderungen im psychischen Befund (wenn es welche gibt, z. B. aktuelle Suizidgefährdung)" and "Veränderungen in der Behandlungsentwicklung". Renewed Aufklärung and Einwilligung are added when the approach changes ([PTK NRW Empfehlungen](https://www.ptk-nrw.de/fileadmin/user_upload/meldungen/2020/Empfehlungen_Dok_Internet.pdf); [BPtK version via PTK Bayern](https://www.ptk-bayern.de/ptk/web.nsf/gfx/med_fdih-bypb6c_ad283/$file/Empfehlungen-der-BPtK-fuer-die-Dokumentation-psychotherapeutischer-Behandlungen-in-der-psychotherapeutischen-Versorgung.pdf)). The document states explicitly that not every point needs filling every session, and that **changes need recording only when they occur**. This matters for the design: an empty field or "keine Veränderung" is valid content.

Training clinics and practitioner templates add the behavioural-therapy homework cycle. The Würzburg university outpatient clinic form runs "Ergebnis der letzten Hausaufgabe(n)", then "Thema/en der Sitzung", "Ergebnis(se) der Sitzung", "Neue Hausaufgabe(n)", and finally "Besondere Absprachen (Suizidalität, FA-Termin, Klinikaufenthalt)" ([Uni Würzburg](https://psychologie.uni-wuerzburg.de/fileadmin/06020160/WAPP/Downloads/Formblaetter/Stundenprotokoll_Supervisionsprotokoll.pdf)). A template circulating as "Sitzungsprotokoll nach DPtV" adds "Veränderungen der aktuellen Lebenssituation", "Interaktionsverhalten" and "Planung der nächsten Sitzung". It is known only from a third-party reproduction ([Heidi Health](https://www.heidihealth.com/en-gb/templates/psychotherapeutisches-sitzungsprotokoll-nach-dptv-2a49b16a)). The sources converge strongly, so a common skeleton is safe. The BPtK keeps the recommendations method-neutral and routes all method-specific content into the single "Verständnis" field. No published German per-session template exists for psychodynamic or systemic therapy. Method-specific prompts, such as Übertragung and Abwehr for TP/AP or Auftragsklärung and Hypothesen for systemic therapy, are therefore inferred from method theory and not from a Kammer form.

The style is terse. Entries are **keyword-style and bulleted under headings**. Session events are written in Präteritum or Perfekt ("berichtete", "zeigte sich"), agreements in Präsens or with "soll", and the client is referred to in the third person ("Pat." or written out) ([Heidi Health DPtV](https://www.heidihealth.com/en-gb/templates/psychotherapeutisches-sitzungsprotokoll-nach-dptv-2a49b16a); [PTK Berlin](https://psychotherapeutenkammer-berlin.de/media/727)). No study measures the typical length. The researchers estimate roughly 80 to 250 words. SOAP (also "SOEP" in German medicine) is taught in Allgemeinmedizin, but no Kammer, KBV or G-BA source prescribes it for psychotherapy. DAP and BIRP appear only on translated US vendor pages ([DocCheck SOAP](https://flexikon.doccheck.com/de/SOAP); [Carepatron DE](https://www.carepatron.com/de/blog/mental-health-progress-note-templates/)). The German tools that compete in this market build around the Verlaufsdokumentation and the KBV workflow, not SOAP ([EPIKUR KI](https://www.epikur.de/ki/); [Doculina](https://www.doculina.de/faq)).

GKV paperwork is a separate class of document that is triggered by events. It includes the PTV forms (PTV 1 application, PTV 2 therapist data, PTV 11 individual client information at the end of the Sprechstunde, PTV 12 notification of acute treatment or therapy end), the Konsiliarbericht (Muster 22), and the Bericht an die Gutachterin oder den Gutachter structured along PTV 3 ([KBV Psychotherapie](https://www.kbv.de/praxis/patientenversorgung/psychotherapie); [KVBB Ausfüllhilfe](https://www.kvbb.de/fileadmin/user_upload/pdf/Genehmigungspflichtige_Leistungen/Psychotherapie/ptv_vordrucke_-_ausfuellhilfe.pdf)). **The Gutachterverfahren still exists in 2026.** The G-BA decided on 19 March 2026 to digitalize it rather than abolish it ([G-BA Pressemitteilung](https://www.g-ba.de/presse/pressemitteilungen-meldungen/1317/)). Abolition is tied to the QS-Verfahren, which runs as a regional trial in Nordrhein and Westfalen-Lippe from 2025 to 2030. The BPtK criticizes it for requiring over 100 data fields per patient. It collects **case-level data at therapy start and end, not per-session content** ([KBV QS](https://www.kbv.de/praxis/tools-und-services/praxisnachrichten/2024/09-05/QS-Verfahren%20zur%20ambulanten%20Psychotherapie-%20Erste%20Stufe%20der%20Erprobung%20startet); [BPtK PM 2024](https://api.bptk.de/uploads/20240123_pm_bptk_QS_Verfahren_ambulante_Psychotherapie_c5fa2332ba.pdf)). The session note feeds these documents only indirectly, as the source material a therapist draws on when writing a Gutachter report or an Epikrise. The ePA is also separate. Psychotherapists fill it only with documents such as the Befundbericht, they must point out the right to object for mental-health data, and they must document any objection in the treatment record ([KBV ePA 2025](https://www.kbv.de/praxis/tools-und-services/praxisnachrichten/2025/08-14/alles-nur-eine-frage-wie-nutzen-psychotherapeuten-die-epa)).

## Scribes err rarely but badly, and the errors cluster where judgment lives

The way AI drafting works is now standard across the German market. EPIKUR (TINA), Doculina, Clara Health, Psynex and Noa Notes all record only with consent. They transcribe the audio, have an LLM fill a German template, present the result as a "Dokumentationsvorschlag" for "Prüfung und Freigabe", and delete the audio after processing. Clara keeps it up to 48 hours to allow a retry. They host in the EU or Germany and promise not to train on customer data ([EPIKUR](https://www.epikur.de/ki/); [Clara Health](https://clarahealth.de/ki-dokumentation-psychotherapie); [Noa](https://noa.ai/de/praxiswissen/blog/ki-dokumentation-fuer-psychotherapeuten)). EPIKUR keeps transcripts for 30 days and claims to save about 20 minutes per protocol. Review is enforced only through the user interface, with a draft status and an approve button. No vendor described a technical lock on export. Psynex also offers ICD-10 suggestions, treatment plans and suicidality screening, without any MDR claim ([Psynex](https://psynex.de/de/dokumentationssoftware-psychotherapie)). This product must not copy those features.

The best primary data on quality comes from Asgari et al. (npj Digital Medicine 2025). They annotated 12,999 sentences in 450 GPT-4 notes and found **1.47% hallucinated sentences, 44% of them major**, and **3.45% of transcript facts omitted, 16.7% of those major**. Major hallucinations concentrated in the Plan (21%) and Assessment (10.5%) sections. By type, 43% were fabrications, 30% negations (the note contradicts what was said), 17% contextual mixing and 10% causal speculation ([Asgari et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12075489/)). The same study found two effective mitigations. Structured per-section output with an explicit "unknown" value cut major hallucinations from 4 to 1, and JSON output with iterative refinement cut major omissions from 61 to 0. Chain-of-thought "atomisation" made results worse. The authors developed the work with a scribe vendor, which is a conflict of interest to keep in mind. Kaiser Permanente's rollout across 303,266 encounters illustrates the typical psychotherapy-relevant error: a prostate exam that had only been discussed for scheduling was documented as performed ([KP Division of Research](https://divisionofresearch.kaiserpermanente.org/publications/ambient-artificial-intelligence-scribes-to-alleviate-the-burden-of-clinical-documentation/); [AMA](https://www.ama-assn.org/practice-management/digital/ai-scribe-saves-doctors-hour-keyboard-every-day)). Errors upstream carry through to the note. Errors seeded into interpreted conversations ended up in 55 to 60% of notes, and omissions carried through most often ([PMC13412140](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13412140/)). In the app, a diarization mistake becomes a misattributed statement.

**No peer-reviewed study measures AI psychotherapy-note accuracy against session transcripts, and none exists for German.** The psychiatry evidence is limited to simulation pilots and a registered trial that has not reported results ([NCT07777224](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07777224)). One primary-care cohort found that AI scribes change *what* gets documented: they recorded more neuropsychiatric symptoms but fewer depression interventions ([PMC12824846](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12824846/)). Psychotherapy notes are mostly interpretive, so the general-medicine error rates likely understate the risk for the Verständnis, Befund and Entwicklung fields. That is a reasoned extrapolation, not a measured result. Two preprints add warnings that are less certain. In one, 21.4% of AI draft sections contained stigmatizing language, and clinicians added more of it than they removed ([arXiv 2606.00019](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.00019)). The other reports that LLM judges are blind to omissions ([arXiv 2608.31016](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.31016)). Two consequences follow. Evaluation needs a check of transcript-to-note coverage, not only verification that each note sentence is supported. And the language checks should also run on the therapist's edits.

## The regulatory line runs between documenting and assessing

German guidance accepts AI documentation. The BPtK Praxis-Info "Administrative KI in Ihrer Praxis", presented around the 48th Deutscher Psychotherapeutentag in May 2026, defines **"Transkripte von Audioaufnahmen" and "Dokumentation in der Patientenakte" as administrative KI**, meaning AI that does not support diagnosis, indication or treatment. It attaches these conditions: the therapist must "das Ergebnis des KI-Systems kritisch prüfen und eigenverantwortlich entscheiden", consent is always required when AI processes health data (written form recommended), processing takes place in the EU/EEA or an adequacy country, data is encrypted or anonymized against the cloud provider, recordings must be deletable, and cloud services need a current C5-Testat under §393 SGB V ([BPtK Praxis-Info](https://api.bptk.de/uploads/bptk_praxisinfo_Administrative_KI_f7ee131776.pdf)). The text was only partly extractable, so a few sentences are reconstructed. The BPtK warns that the boundary is not sharp and that "entscheidend ist nicht die Zweckbestimmung der KI, sondern die tatsächliche Anwendung". At the PTK NRW's December 2025 event, medical-law professor Sigrid Lorz said diagnostics, indication and treatment planning cannot be delegated. She also said therapists bear liability when they adopt AI output without checking it ([PTK NRW Ratschlag](https://www.ptk-nrw.de/aktuelles/meldungen/detail/rueckblick-auf-den-grossen-ratschlag-kuenstliche-intelligenz-ki-und-psychotherapie-am-3-dezember-2025)).

The product rules against clinical suggestions, emotion detection and risk detection fit the EU regulatory boundaries. Under MDCG 2019-11 (Rev. 1, 2025), whether software is a medical device depends on its intended purpose. Software that acts on data to support diagnosis or treatment of an individual is typically **MDR class IIa or higher under Rule 11**, which requires a notified body ([MDCG 2019-11](https://health.ec.europa.eu/document/download/b45335c5-1679-4c71-a91c-fc7a4d37f12b_en?filename=md_mdcg_2019_11_guidance_qualification_classification_software_en.pdf)). The UK's MHRA clarified in July 2026 that ambient voice technology used only to transcribe, summarize or draft for clinician review is not a medical device. Products that support diagnosis or act without review are regulated ([MHRA](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/mhra-clarifies-regulatory-status-of-ambient-voice-technologies-used-in-the-nhs)). The EU has issued no equivalent ruling, but the logic carries over. Writing down a diagnosis the therapist stated is documentation. Proposing one is decision support. Under the EU AI Act, a documentation-only scribe is not high-risk. It would become high-risk under Art. 6(1) only as a notified-body medical device, and the Digital Omnibus moved those obligations to **2 August 2028** ([Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/)). The publication of the Omnibus in the Official Journal is reported by one industry source only. Emotion recognition systems are a separate Annex III high-risk category and trigger the Art. 50(3) disclosure duty. The ban on emotion detection should therefore extend to the text: the draft must not infer affect ("wirkte ängstlich") from voice or wording.

Recording and data processing add criminal-law layers. §8 Abs. 7 MBO requires **prior consent** for sound recordings and requires informing the client of their right to demand deletion. Recording without authorization is punishable under §201 StGB with up to three years ([BPtK MBO](https://api.bptk.de/uploads/Muster_Berufsordnung_der_B_Pt_K_412a6bcb36.pdf); [§201 StGB](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stgb/__201.html)). Consent is needed from everyone in the room, which covers couples, families and the parents of minors. Since 2017, §203 Abs. 3 StGB has allowed disclosure to cloud and AI providers as "mitwirkende Personen". Under Abs. 4, however, the therapist is liable if the provider and its subcontractors are not bound to secrecy. An Art. 28 GDPR processing agreement does not cover that ([§203 StGB](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stgb/__203.html); [Bitkom Muster](https://www.bitkom.org/sites/main/files/file/import/20180718-Muster-203StGB-final.pdf)). For Gemini on Vertex AI, Google contractually does not train on customer data. Zero retention still requires disabling the 24-hour in-memory cache, requesting the abuse-monitoring logging exception, and avoiding Search grounding, which keeps data for three days and cannot be turned off ([Vertex AI data governance](https://docs.cloud.google.com/vertex-ai/generative-ai/docs/data-governance)). One tension remains unresolved. The BPtK expects data to be protected against the provider, but LLM inference needs plaintext. The data protection impact assessment (DSFA) has to address this, for example through pseudonymization and the §203 contractual route, and counsel should confirm the approach. One more open point: no Kammer or court has said whether a retained transcript is part of the Patientenakte. If it is kept, it is safest to treat it as inspectable and as subject to the 10-year retention.

## A recommended note template: "Sitzungsdokumentation (Verlaufsdokumentation)"

The template follows the BPtK fields so German reviewers and Kammern recognize it. It adds the homework and planning fields that practice templates share. Each field is tagged with who may write it. "KI-Entwurf" fields are drafted only from attributed transcript content and the therapist's note chips. "Nur Therapeut:in" fields are never pre-filled by the model. At most they show the therapist's own chip text verbatim, marked as such. The split follows the evidence that major errors concentrate in assessment and plan sections. It also follows the MDR and AI Act boundary and the product's three prohibitions.

## Design implications for the app

**Generation pipeline.** The model receives the diarized transcript and the therapist's note chips. It returns structured JSON per field. Each statement has the form `{text, source_refs[], speaker, kind: reported|intervention|agreement|plan}`, and a field with nothing to say returns an explicit `status: "nicht besprochen"`. This is the one pattern with trial evidence for fewer major hallucinations and omissions. Fields 7–10 are not in the model's output schema at all. Enforcing that in the schema, rather than in a prompt instruction, is what keeps the product inside its intended purpose, and it holds up in an MDR or AI Act review. Therapist chips rank above the transcript. When the two conflict, the app shows the conflict and does not resolve it silently. The prompt has to separate *discussed*, *planned* and *done* (the Kaiser Permanente prostate-exam error), and *client reported* from *therapist said*. Hedged reporting ("berichtet") is used for the client's statements. The model should not emit evaluative or affect adjectives: by default it does not write "wirkte gedrückt" and quotes the client's own words instead. Third parties appear as roles ("Partner", "Mutter"), not names. That keeps the note within the minimum necessary and avoids later §630g redactions for third-party rights. A second verification pass marks unsupported sentences. A separate coverage check lists transcript content that did not make it into the note, under "evtl. relevant, nicht übernommen". The model never drafts the suicidality field. If the therapist leaves it empty, the signed note says nothing about it, rather than an implied "keine Suizidalität". Any risk content in the record therefore comes from the therapist alone.

**Review and signing UX.** The draft carries a persistent "KI-Entwurf – nicht geprüft" label. A side-by-side view lets the therapist click a sentence to see its transcript segment. Unsupported statements and flagged language (for example "verweigert", "manipulativ", "behauptet") must each be accepted, edited or deleted before signing. That friction is a deliberate counter to automation bias, although no study has yet measured its effect. The therapist-only fields show short prompts that can be skipped ("Veränderungen im Befund?", "Suizidalität thematisiert?"). They help against omissions and the §630h presumption without the model making any assessment. The language check also runs on the therapist's own edits. The app should nudge the therapist to sign the same or the next day, which is the Kammer's reading of "zeitnah", for example by showing unsigned drafts on the Today screen.

**Record integrity.** Signing is the legal act. It stamps the signer, the time, the template version and the AI-assistance marker. After signing, the record is append-only: a correction is a new signed "Nachtrag" version, and the original stays readable with its date, as §630f Abs. 1 and §9 Abs. 1 MBO require. The architecture encrypts signed records with OpenPGP so the server cannot read them. Versioning, the full export for a §630g/Art. 15 request (first copy free) and any justified redaction must therefore run in the therapist's browser. The record must stay decryptable for 10 years after treatment ends, including after practice closure (§24 MBO), so the recovery key is a compliance feature, not an optional extra. Storing the diff between AI draft and signed note gives QA data, but the diff contains client data. It needs the same encryption and its own retention rule, and if it is kept it should be treated as inspectable.

**Retention and consent.** Audio is deleted at signing, as already planned. The transcript should also be deleted at signing or after a short configurable period, and the remaining time should be visible to the therapist. A retained transcript is inspectable by the client and could be argued to fall under the 10-year retention duty. This is the strongest reason to keep the signed note as the only permanent content in the Akte. The consent texts should name the purpose (AI-assisted drafting), the processors (including Google as an Art. 28 subprocessor bound under §203 StGB), the deletion periods and the right to demand deletion. Consent is needed from every person present. A "ohne Aufnahme" path must not disadvantage the client: the therapist writes the same template manually, and the AI fields remain available for dictated chips only. On Vertex, the app should stay in the EU region, keep caching disabled, never use grounding, and pursue the abuse-monitoring exception. The DSFA has to address the plaintext-at-inference tension explicitly. The product should also give practices AI-literacy material (Art. 4 AI Act) and a DSFA template, because the BPtK checklist expects both.

**Scope of documents.** The per-session note is one document class among several. Basisdokumentation, PTV 11 output, the Bericht an den Gutachter, the Befundbericht or Arztbrief, the Epikrise and the QS fields each have their own structure and trigger. A later feature that drafts a PTV 3 report from signed notes would be administrative KI by the BPtK definition only if it assembles what the therapist documented. It must not generate the "verfahrensspezifisches Störungsmodell" or a prognosis. Marketing and help texts must describe the product as "Dokumentationsentwurf" and never as "Einschätzung", because regulators read the intended purpose from labelling and promotional material.

## Conclusion

For this product, the German record format and the EU regulatory boundary point to the same design. The BPtK template already separates narrative from interpretation. The scribe evidence shows that AI errors concentrate in interpretation. And MDR, the AI Act and the BPtK's definition of "administrative KI" all treat interpretation as the line that turns a tool into a regulated clinical product. A note in which the AI drafts the narrative fields and the therapist writes the judgment fields is safer, compliant, and also closer to what Kammern consider good documentation, because it makes the therapist's hypothesis-driven reasoning visible instead of hiding it behind fluent machine prose. Three gaps remain, and the product should not rely on any of them. There is no German or psychotherapy-specific accuracy data, so the team needs its own German gold set of role-play sessions labeled for hallucinations and omissions. The legal status of retained transcripts is unsettled. And the final BPtK guidance on diagnostic and treatment AI is still pending. Keeping transcripts short-lived and the model out of the clinical fields means none of these open questions can turn into a liability.
